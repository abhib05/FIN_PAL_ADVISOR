FinPal helps you understand your money.

A budget is a plan for your income and your expenses. Every rupee you earn
should have a job: rent, food, savings, or fun. When you track your spending
for a month, patterns appear. Small subscriptions add up. Eating out adds up.
The first step to a healthy budget is simply knowing where your money goes.

An emergency fund is money set aside for surprises: a medical bill, a job
loss, a broken laptop. A good target is three to six months of essential
expenses. Keep this money in a savings account you can access quickly, not
locked away in stocks or fixed deposits.

A Systematic Investment Plan, or SIP, lets you invest a fixed amount every
month into a mutual fund. Over many years, compounding turns small monthly
contributions into a large corpus. The earlier you start, the more time
compounding has to work in your favor. Time in the market matters more than
timing the market.

An EMI is an equated monthly installment, the fixed payment you make each
month to repay a loan. Before taking a loan, check that your total EMIs stay
well under forty percent of your monthly income, so you still have room for
savings and emergencies.

Debt payoff strategies come in two common flavors. The avalanche method pays
off the debt with the highest interest rate first, saving the most money
over time. The snowball method pays off the smallest balance first, building
momentum and motivation. Either method beats making only minimum payments.

Your FI number is the amount of savings you need for financial independence,
usually estimated as twenty five times your annual expenses, based on a four
percent safe withdrawal rate. Reaching your FI number does not mean you must
stop working; it means working becomes a choice rather than a necessity.

Subscriptions and buy now pay later plans can quietly drain a budget. Review
your recurring charges every few months and cancel what you no longer use.
Small monthly leaks, left unchecked, become large yearly losses.

Diversification means spreading your money across different kinds of
investments, so that a single bad outcome does not sink your whole plan.
Stocks, bonds, gold, and real estate each behave differently when markets
move, and a mix tends to smooth out the ride.

A rupee saved today is worth more than a rupee saved tomorrow, because it can
be invested and grow. Start small, stay consistent, and let time do the
heavy lifting. FinPal is here to help you turn financial goals into a plan,
and a plan into action.
